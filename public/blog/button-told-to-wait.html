No. 07 · Ownership

The button that was told to wait

The game said it was your turn, the PASS button sat there looking pressable, and pressing it did nothing whatsoever — from the first second of every game we ever played.

Every time somebody opens ZA!, the game phones the kitchen. For that first instant it knows nothing yet, so it politely switches four things off: PASS, CALL OUT, ZA!, and the pile you draw from. That is the right instinct. Nothing should be pressable while the game is still saying hello.

Then the line connects, and all four should come back on.

Two of them did. Two of them never did, ever, not once.

So who turns it back on?

The screen redraws itself constantly — every card played, every turn passed. And each time it redraws, it works out from scratch whether you are allowed to shout ZA! and whether you are allowed to draw, then sets those two controls to match. So yes, the connecting screen switched them off. And the very next redraw switched them straight back on, without anyone planning it. They healed by accident.

PASS and CALL OUT were built differently. The redraw only ever decided whether to show them or hide them. It never once said whether they could be pressed. So the “still connecting” switch flicked them off, and after that nothing in the entire game ever flicked them back.

That is the whole trap. The code that switched them off was written in the belief that somebody else would switch them back on. Nobody had ever agreed to.

We watched it happen

This is not a theory about the code. We sat down at a real table, with a real deal, on our own turn. The sign across the top of the screen read “Your turn — play it or pass”. The button was right there, full size, looking exactly like every working button in the game. We pressed it and not a single message left the building. The only way to end that turn was to sit still and let the clock run out.

The spare entrance

Here is the part that kept this hidden for weeks. You can also call a chef out by clicking on them where they sit — the button is not the only way in. So calling out kept working beautifully. Anyone who used the chef never learned the button was dead, and anyone who tried the button assumed they had simply been too slow.

PASS has no second door. That is the only reason we ever found this at all.

The fix is dull, which is usually the sign of a good one. On every redraw the game now states, out loud, for all four controls: whether the move exists, and whether it can be made. Nothing sits waiting to be rescued by somebody else.

HOUSE RULE

If two pieces of code share one switch, neither of them owns it — give every switch exactly one owner. And a spare entrance will happily hide a locked front door.
