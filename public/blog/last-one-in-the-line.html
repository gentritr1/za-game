The last one in the line

The row of chefs re-sorted itself and every one of them slid smoothly across to their new seat — except the last one, who simply teleported.

When the order of play changes, the chefs along the counter have to re-sort. We did not want them blinking into new places; that is disorienting, and you lose track of who is who. So they walk instead. Each chef is put straight back where they were standing a moment ago, then let go, and the browser slides them across to where they now belong. It is a lovely trick and it costs almost nothing.

It worked for everybody except whoever happened to be last in the row. They arrived in their new seat instantly, with no journey at all, every single time.

The browser keeps a notepad

Here is the thing you need to know, and it is really the whole post.

When you tell a browser to move something, it does not move it there and then. It jots the instruction on a notepad and gets to it in a moment, along with everything else you have asked for, because doing the whole list in one go is enormously faster than doing each one as it arrives.

But the second you ask it a question — where is this thing, how wide is it — the notepad is no use. It cannot answer you out of a list of intentions. So it stops, does everything it was putting off, and only then measures.

Which means that asking “where is this?” quietly also means “finish everything you were saving up”.

Off by one

Our code walked down the row one chef at a time, doing two things to each: measure where they are right now, then write down where they should end up. Read that back with the notepad in mind:

  • measuring chef 2 finishes chef 1’s move
  • measuring chef 3 finishes chef 2’s move
  • measuring chef 4 finishes chef 3’s move
  • …and then we write chef 4’s move, and nobody measures anything ever again

Chef 4’s instruction was still sitting on the notepad, unread, at the moment the animation began. So chef 4 was never actually put back where they came from. They were just already in the new seat, with nothing behind them to slide from.

One more question, asked once

The fix is a single line, placed after the loop instead of inside it: ask the browser one deliberately pointless question about the row. That question clears the notepad, chef 4’s move lands like everybody else’s, and the whole line slides together.

The family resemblance

This is the second time this zine has caught the same shape. Over in Two pizzas at once, one mural covered the one beneath it — not because anything in the code said so, but as a free side effect of the pictures being solid. Nobody had asked for it, and it quietly stopped the day the pictures went see-through.

Same story here. Each measurement was finishing the previous chef’s work for free. Nobody had asked for that either, and it ran out exactly one chef early.

HOUSE RULE

If you measure and change in the same loop, the final change is still floating when the loop ends. Measure once more, after it.
