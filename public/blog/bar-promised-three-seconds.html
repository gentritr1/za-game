No. 08 · Honesty

The bar that promised three seconds

A little bar on screen drained away over three seconds, so everybody believed they had three seconds. Nowhere in the rules of ZA! is there any such thing.

Here is the bit of the game this is about. When a chef gets down to their last card, they are supposed to shout ZA! If they forget, anybody at the table can catch them and make them pick cards back up. Catching someone is called calling them out.

While that chance was open, the screen drew a small bar under the guilty chef, and the bar drained from full to empty in five neat steps over three seconds. Nobody needed that explained. Hurry — this is nearly gone.

It was not nearly gone. It was not going anywhere.

Nobody had ever written a three

We went looking for the timer behind the window, and there is no timer. We read the whole rules file hunting for anything that so much as glances at a clock, and found exactly one thing: a line that stamps the time onto a message in the game log, so you can see when it happened. That is the lot. Not a deadline anywhere in the game.

The real rule is a shape, not a stopwatch. You are catchable from the moment you play your second-to-last card until play comes all the way back round to you. At a busy table that is easily half a minute. We watched at six seconds — still open. At thirty — still open.

So for twenty-seven of those thirty seconds the screen had been quietly telling everyone the chance was gone while it was in fact wide open. The picture had invented a deadline the game does not have, and players were politely obeying it.

The fix is that it stops moving

You would expect us to correct the number — make it five seconds, or ten, or count down the real thing. But the honest length cannot be known in advance. It depends entirely on what everyone else does next, and it is different every single time.

So the bar kept its shape, its colour and its place, and simply stopped draining. It sits there, full, for exactly as long as the chance is open, and vanishes the instant it closes. How long it stays is the answer now. Nothing moves, so nothing can hint at an ending.

The little run of rising beeps went with it, for the same reason: that was a countdown in sound, counting to the same deadline that was never there. One note still plays when the window opens, because a chance nobody notices is its own kind of lie.

Which is also why this post has a still picture where you might expect an animation. The stillness is the fix.

HOUSE RULE

Never let the picture promise something the rules do not keep. If there is no clock, nothing on screen may drain.
