No. 09 · Design

The waiter who never learns

Somebody wanders off. The table waits three quarters of a minute for them, plays their turn for them — and then waits the entire three quarters of a minute again on their next turn. And the next. And the next.

ZA! is played at a counter, in order, round and round. If it is your turn and you do nothing at all, the table eventually gets on with it and plays a card for you so everyone else is not stuck. That wait is 45 seconds.

Forty-five is a deliberate, generous number, and we like it. It is there to give you the benefit of the doubt. Maybe you are thinking. Maybe you got up for a drink. Maybe your hand is a disaster and you are counting your options twice. You should not be hustled out of your own turn by a machine that is bored.

The trouble was that the table never remembered doing it.

Your turn comes round again: another 45 seconds. And again: another 45. Somebody who had shut their laptop ten minutes ago was still being handed the full benefit of the doubt, over and over, forever.

What that costs the people who stayed

Sit four chefs at the counter and let one of them wander off. A round of ZA! runs about ten laps of the table. Ten laps at 45 seconds of nothing each is seven and a half minutes of three real people watching a screen do absolutely nothing — in a game where taking your turn takes about two seconds.

And here is the sharp bit. The table already knew. It found out the very first time: those first 45 seconds ended with nobody home. That is not a suspicion any more, it is an answer — and the game went on paying for the question regardless.

Pay for the doubt once

The benefit of the doubt is worth real money. It is the thing that stops a game feeling like it is shoving you along. So we did not take it away — we just stopped buying it over and over.

The first time you go quiet you get the full 45 seconds, exactly as before. Nothing at all changes for somebody who is simply thinking. But if that wait actually runs out, the table has learned something real, and each of your turns after that gets about 12 seconds instead.

And the moment you touch anything — anything at all — you are a full-price player again, instantly. There is no penalty box, no grudge, nothing to earn back. You are just back.

Same four chefs, same absent player: three minutes of dead air becomes about eighty seconds. Nobody who was actually playing can tell the difference.

HOUSE RULE

The benefit of the doubt is worth paying for once, not every time. A wait that has already run out has answered the question — stop charging everyone else to ask it again.
