The column that would not shrink

1fr does not mean “one equal share”. It means “one equal share, but never narrower than what is inside you” — and that second half is where your layout goes.

Lay four seats out in a row with repeat(4, 1fr) and you get four equal columns. Then one player types a long nickname, picks up a HOST badge, and their column is suddenly wider than the other three. Your CSS did not change. Your data did.

Why a fraction is not a fraction

1fr is shorthand. What you actually wrote was minmax(auto, 1fr), and that auto is a minimum: the track may never be narrower than its content’s min-content size — roughly, the widest thing in there that cannot be broken up, like an unbreakable word or a chip with padding on it.

So the moment one cell’s content is wider than its fair share, that track stops being a fair share. It becomes “whatever this content demands”, and the other three columns divide whatever is left.

1fr only distributes the space the content is willing to give up. Usually the content is not willing.

Say the minimum out loud

One chip resizes the row

grid-template-columns:
  repeat(4, 1fr);
/* = minmax(auto, 1fr) */

Four equal columns

grid-template-columns:
  repeat(4, minmax(0, 1fr));

minmax(0, 1fr) states the minimum, and states that it is zero. Now the track really is an equal share and the content is what has to give way: text wraps, a long name gets an ellipsis, an overflowing strip scrolls inside its own box. That is what you wanted all along. You just had to say who yields.

There is a bonus here that catches people out separately: overflow: hidden and text-overflow: ellipsis inside a grid child do nothing at all while the track is being sized to the content they were supposed to clip. Fix the track and the clipping starts working, as if by magic. It was never magic.

The same rule, one axis over

Our lobby lays eight seats out in a tray. It read as two rows of different-sized tiles — but only once somebody actually sat down.

The tiles had a min-height, and a seated chef (avatar, name, a HOST or CPU badge) grew past it. A grid row is as tall as its tallest tenant, so only the tiles sharing that row stretched. The tray was sizing itself item by item.

We nearly raised the min-height until it covered the tallest case. That works, right up until the next thing that grows a seat.

The tray owns the height

.seat-list {
  display: grid;
  grid-template-columns: repeat(4, 1fr);
  grid-auto-rows: 1fr;   /* not min-height on the tile */
}

One line. Now a nickname chip grows every seat together, or none of them. The module owns the height; the item does not.

Both halves are the same decision. Somebody has to yield — the container or the content — and if you do not say which, the content wins by default and takes the layout with it.

HOUSE RULE

Reach for minmax(0, 1fr) when the columns should be equal, and grid-auto-rows: 1fr when the module should own the height — never the item.
